Below is a **step-by-step vertical jump protocol** that breaks down **every limb/body part** and what it should do in each phase. It’s written so you can literally “run through” it mentally or physically.

I’ll describe:
- **Standing / Pre-jump**
- **Counter-movement (loading)**
- **Push-off (propulsion)**
- **Flight**
- **Landing**

Then I’ll add a **training protocol** (phases, frequency, progressive steps) at the end.

***

## 1. Standing / Pre-Jump Position (Ready)

Goal: Neutral, stable, spring-ready posture.

- **Head & neck**
  - Neutral alignment; eyes forward.
  - No jutting chin or tilting head back/down. [a1athlete](https://a1athlete.com/vertical-jump-technique/)

- **Torso / spine**
  - Upright, chest up, slight natural lumbar curve.
  - Hips aligned above ankles; no leaning forward/backward.

- **Arms**
  - Arms relaxed, hanging slightly behind or beside hips.
  - Elbows soft (not locked), shoulders down, not hunched.

- **Pelvis**
  - Neutral tilt (not excessive anterior or posterior tilt).
  - Slight engagement of core to stabilize.

- **Hips**
  - Near full extension (standing), but not “locked.”
  - Muscles pre-activated (glutes, hip extensors lightly engaged).

- **Knees**
  - Near full extension, soft (not hyperextended).
  - Knees aligned over feet, not collapsing inward.

- **Ankles & feet**
  - Feet flat, roughly hip-width apart.
  - Slight weight on midfoot, not just toes or heels.
  - Ankles neutral (no extreme dorsiflexion/plantarflexion).

- **Eyes**
  - Fixed on a target (e.g., rim, wall mark) to guide intent.

***

## 2. Counter-Movement (Loading Phase)

Goal: Quick, controlled downward motion to load elastic energy and activate muscles.

Think: “Explode down, then explode up.”

- **Head & neck**
  - Stay neutral; no head-back or chin-up.
  - Visual focus stays on target.

- **Torso**
  - Maintain upright posture; do not “hinge” or dive forward at hips. [a1athlete](https://a1athlete.com/vertical-jump-technique/)
  - Slight posterior lean is okay, but chest stays up.

- **Arms**
  - Arms swing **down and slightly back** in coordination with lower body.
  - Elbows stay soft; shoulders relax but stay engaged.

- **Pelvis**
  - Moves down as hips flex.
  - Avoid excessive forward pelvic tilt.

- **Hips**
  - Begin flexing (bending) as you drop.
  - Glutes stretch; hip extensors loaded.

- **Knees**
  - Begin flexing (bending) smoothly.
  - Knee flexion ~30–60° depending on depth and style.
  - Knees stay aligned with toes (no inward collapse).

- **Ankles**
  - Begin dorsiflexing (foot moves toward shin).
  - ankles absorb load, tendons stretch.

- **Feet**
  - Pressure remains on midfoot.
  - Slight forward shift of load toward balls of feet as you load.

Timing:
- This phase is **fast**, not slow: ~0.25–0.35 s in trained athletes. [youtube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=huSCrUiOHfA)
- No pause between loading and push-off.

***

## 3. Push-Off (Propulsion Phase)

Goal: Max upward acceleration via explosive joint extension and arm swing.

This is the actual “jump” part.

### 3.1 Big Picture Coordination

- Movement starts from **hips → knees → ankles** (proximal-to-distal).
- Arms swing **up aggressively** in sync with lower body.
- Entire body acts as one spring: load → release.

### 3.2 Body Part Details

- **Head & neck**
  - Still neutral, eyes up to target.
  - No head-back; no excessive chin-up.

- **Torso**
  - Extend slightly upward as hips drive up.
  - Chest stays up; avoid “hollowing” or collapsing at the core.

- **Arms**
  - Swing **up and forward** from hips to overhead.
  - Elbows extend, shoulders flex (raise arms).
  - At takeoff, arms reach near full overhead extension.
  - This adds upward momentum and helps balance COM.

- **Pelvis**
  - Drives upward as hips extend.
  - Pelvis tilts slightly anteriorly as hips thrust up.

- **Hips**
  - Rapid **extension** (straightening).
  - Glutes, hamstrings, adductors produce power.
  - Hip extension is the largest contributor to jump power in many athletes. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16338725/)

- **Knees**
  - Rapid **extension** (straightening).
  - Quadriceps produce power.
  - Knee extension follows hip extension.

- **Ankles**
  - Rapid **plantarflexion** (pushing toes down).
  - Gastroc/soleus push through the forefoot.
  - Ankle is the “final push” that adds height.

- **Feet**
  - Push through midfoot → forefoot.
  - At takeoff, only toes/forefoot contact ground.
  - Full extension through ankle = “push through the toes.”

Takeoff:
- When feet leave ground, all joints are near full extension.
- Arms are high overhead.
- Head/neck neutral, eyes on target.

***

## 4. Flight Phase

Goal: Control body position; no more force can be added to COM.

You cannot increase jump height now, but you can optimize clearance and technique.

- **Head & neck**
  - Neutral, eyes still on target.
  - No hyperextension or tilting.

- **Torso**
  - Upright, slightly extended.
  - Core engaged to maintain stable posture.

- **Arms**
  - Fully extended overhead or slightly forward.
  - Keep arms straight, not flailing.

- **Pelvis & hips**
  - Hips near full extension.
  - Pelvis stable, not rotating excessively.

- **Knees**
  - Near full extension.
  - In some styles (e.g., basketball), slight knee flexion may occur mid-flight for prep, but COM path is fixed.

- **Ankles & feet**
  - Ankles in plantarflexion (toes down).
  - Legs straight, feet relaxed but pointed.

If you want to maximize clear height (e.g., for a dunk or reach):
- Extend fully: head up, arms up, legs straight.
- Avoid unnecessary movements that shift segments but don’t change COM path.

***

## 5. Landing Phase

Goal: Absorb impact safely, protect knees and spine.

- **Head & neck**
  - Neutral; eyes forward.
  - No head-jerk or looking down excessively.

- **Torso**
  - Slight forward lean is okay to control descent, but chest up.
  - Core engaged to stabilize spine.

- **Arms**
  - Arms move down and slightly back to counterbalance.
  - Helps control COM and reduce forward momentum.

- **Pelvis**
  - Slight posterior tilt as hips flex.
  - Avoid excessive anterior tilt.

- **Hips**
  - Controlled **flexion** (bending).
  - Glutes and hamstrings work eccentrically to absorb.

- **Knees**
  - Controlled **flexion** (bend).
  - Knees align with toes; no inward collapse.
  - Eccentric quadriceps absorb impact.

- **Ankles**
  - Controlled **dorsiflexion**.
  - Move from forefoot to more whole foot contact.

- **Feet**
  - Land softly: first forefoot, then gradually whole foot.
  - Feet stay hip-width; avoid wide or narrow landing.
  - “Stick” landing: pause briefly, maintain balance before next movement. [massgeneral](https://www.massgeneral.org/assets/mgh/pdf/orthopaedics/sports-medicine/physical-therapy/mass-general-jump-and-plyometric-training.pdf)

***

## 6. Full Movement Summary (One Jump)

1. **Stand**: Neutral, upright, arms ready.  
2. **Load**: Quick dip with hips/knees/ankles, arms down/back.  
3. **Explode**: Hips → knees → ankles extend; arms swing up.  
4. **Fly**: Full extension, arms overhead, eyes on target.  
5. **Land**: Soft, controlled flexion; stick landing.

***

## 7. Training Protocol (Step-by-Step Progressive Plan)

This is a **12-week vertical jump protocol** in phases, with limb-specific focus notes.

### General Rules

- Train **2–3 times per week** for jumps/plyos.
- Warm-up: 10–15 min light cardio + dynamic stretching.
- Keep total foot contacts per session:
  - Phase 1: ~60 contacts
  - Phase 2: ~90 contacts
  - Phase 3: ~120 contacts. [massgeneral](https://www.massgeneral.org/assets/mgh/pdf/orthopaedics/sports-medicine/physical-therapy/mass-general-jump-and-plyometric-training.pdf)
- Always land with “stick” technique, knees aligned, no collapse inward. [massgeneral](https://www.massgeneral.org/assets/mgh/pdf/orthopaedics/sports-medicine/physical-therapy/mass-general-jump-and-plyometric-training.pdf)

***

### Phase 1 – Foundation (Weeks 1–3)

Focus: Build basic jumping mechanics, tendon stiffness, and landing control.

**Frequency:** 2 sessions/week.

**Exercises (double-leg, simple):**

1. **Mini-trampoline double-leg hops**
   - 3 × 10 reps.
   - Limb focus:
     - Ankles: quick, small plantarflexion/dorsiflexion.
     - Knees: small flexion/extension, stay aligned.
     - Hips: small flexion/extension, keep torso upright.
     - Arms: small swing, coordinated with legs.

2. **Soft-surface double-leg hops** (gym floor, carpet)
   - 3 × 10 reps.
   - Focus on soft landing and quick rebound.

3. **Jump rope (double-leg)**
   - 2 × 30 seconds.
   - Focus:
     - Ankles: quick, light plantarflexion.
     - Knees: minimal flexion, mostly ankle-driven.
     - Hips & torso: stable, upright.

**Technique checklist per jump:**
- Neutral head, chest up.
- Small counter-movement, no pause.
- Extensions: hips → knees → ankles.
- Land softly, knees aligned, stick landing. [massgeneral](https://www.massgeneral.org/assets/mgh/pdf/orthopaedics/sports-medicine/physical-therapy/mass-general-jump-and-plyometric-training.pdf)

***

### Phase 2 – Power Development (Weeks 4–7)

Focus: Longer, more explosive jumps; full-range mechanics.

**Frequency:** 2–3 sessions/week.

**Exercises:**

1. **Double-leg forward hops (6–12”)**
   - 5 reps.
   - Limb focus:
     - Hips: strong extension, drive forward and up.
     - Knees: greater flexion/extension.
     - Ankles: powerful plantarflexion.
     - Arms: large swing forward/up.

2. **Double-leg side-to-side hops (6–12”)**
   - 5 reps each direction.
   - Focus lateral control, keep knees aligned.

3. **Broad jumps (12–18”)**
   - 5 reps.
   - Full counter-movement:
     - Hips flex deeply, then explode.
     - Arms swing big.
     - Land with full-body flexion, stick.

4. **Broad jump → vertical jump**
   - 5 reps.
   - Sequence:
     - Broad jump forward.
     - Immediately into a vertical jump.
   - Limb coordination:
     - Hips: reset quickly, then extend.
     - Knees/ankles: quick reset and push.
     - Arms: continuous swing.

5. **Jump rope alternating single-leg hops**
   - 30 reps total.
   - Focus ankle strength and balance.

**Technique emphasis:**
- Full counter-movement, no pause.
- Upright torso; no diving forward. [a1athlete](https://a1athlete.com/vertical-jump-technique/)
- Full extension in flight; arms overhead.
- Soft, aligned landings.

***

### Phase 3 – Advanced & Specific (Weeks 8–12)

Focus: Multi-directional, high-intensity, and sport-specific jumps.

**Frequency:** 2–3 sessions/week.
**Rest:** Optional light week at week 8 if needed.

**Exercises:**

1. **90° double-leg jumps**
   - 5 reps each direction.
   - Jump, rotate 90°, land, stick.
   - Limb focus:
     - Hips: rotate and stabilize.
     - Knees: control rotation, avoid collapse.
     - Ankles: absorb and redirect force.

2. **180° double-leg jumps**
   - 5 reps.
   - Same principles, larger rotation.

3. **Broad jump → vertical jump with 90°–180° turn**
   - 5 reps each.
   - Full sequence:
     - Broad jump, turn, vertical jump.
   - Coordination:
     - Hips & torso: rotate then extend.
     - Arms: large swing, help rotation and height.

4. **Single-leg hops (mini-tramp)**
   - 30–60 reps total.
   - Focus:
     - Single-leg ankle strength.
     - Knee stability.
     - Hip control.

5. **Jump rope single-leg (double/triple hops)**
   - Alternating feet.
   - Focus: ankle power, balance.

6. **Single-leg forward & side hops (6–12”)**
   - 5 reps each direction each leg.
   - Emphasize:
     - Single-leg hip extension.
     - Knee alignment.
     - Ankle push.

**Technique rules:**
- Always land softly, knees aligned, no inward collapse.
- Maintain upright torso and neutral head. [a1athlete](https://a1athlete.com/vertical-jump-technique/)
- Full extension in flight; arms overhead.
- Stick landing before next jump.

***

## 8. How to Use This Protocol

1. **Pick a phase** based on experience:
   - Beginner: Phase 1.
   - Intermediate: Phase 2.
   - Advanced: Phase 3.

2. **Each session:**
   - Warm-up 10–15 min.
   - Do the listed jumps.
   - Cool-down: stretch hips, knees, ankles, calves, hamstrings.

3. **Track:**
   - Monthly vertical jump test (standing and with approach).
   - Note any pain or poor mechanics.
